In short

A real river, Dry Creek in the Sierra foothills, is watched by a camera. Every five minutes an AI agent on Modal (Dave's) looks at what the river camera and the gallery camera have sent, reads its own journal, and decides three things: which real day from the creek's past (2002–2023) the piece should relive next, when that day's surge lands, and the colours and mood (a palette and four knobs). It writes them down with a one-sentence reason.

bitcube, the PC in the gallery, reads that decision and makes the light: every five-minute loop replays one remembered day's 24 hours on the twenty ropes and the four screens: calm drips, a building flow, the surge (a white front falling down every rope into a churning pool), then settling. A new day arrives with one slow wave down the ropes. Between the remembered days, about every twenty minutes, the piece plays one cycle of Fill my cup, water rising from the floor to the top and draining out. People standing still near the ropes slow the light and draw it toward them.

The agent chooses and composes; it does not render pixels. bitcube renders; it does not choose the day. If the agent is late or the internet drops, bitcube carries on with a notable day of its own: the piece never stops.

Two things are easy to get wrong in conversation. The agent never sees raw video: each clip becomes a short caption and a few numbers before it reads anything. And the agent's decision does not reach the ropes directly: bitcube reads it from a mirrored file and folds it into the next loop, with easing, clamps and a power limit, so the agent can ask for a mood but can never cause a flash.

The cycle

The order and the counts are the playlist, edited on the Looks tab without restarting anything. Today's playlist is the one drawn above: the remembered river for 4 loops, then Fill my cup (simple) for 1 cycle. The Looks tab always shows the real one, and which look is on the lights this minute.

Only the look on the lights runs. The remembered river's clock keeps wall time while it is hidden, so after the fill it comes back part-way through a loop rather than restarting. A remembered day is never repeated within an hour.

Each input and what it changes

The remembered river (most of the time)

The remembered dayDave's agent, each tick: the day most like today (today's flow, rain, temperature, the date) or, when the river camera is offline or dark or nothing changed, one of 30 notable days (a flood, a drought, the first rains). Chosen with a one-sentence reason.Which day's 24 hours the loop replays: its hourly flow and rain shape how fast the water builds and how heavy the surge is; its season shades the colours (icy winter, teal-green spring); night is greyer and darker; cold is whiter. The surge hourDave's agent (surge_at), else the day's heaviest hour of rain.When the surge lands in the loop, always between 1½ and 3½ minutes in. During opening nights (the default) every loop gets a surge, even on a dry day.
The paletteDave's scene colours.The water's colours: 60 % Dave's palette, 40 % the day's season, always kept cool and saturated. The front of the surge is always white-cyan.
The four knobsDave's scene: speed, density, brightness, turbulence (0–1), with his reflexes nudging them between ticks.How fast the flow runs, how many ropes carry drips, how bright the body of the water is, how rough the surge looks.
"Heard"Dave's reflexes: how loud the river sounds in the latest clip (0–1).The ropes follow the sound when it is fresh: louder water, more flow. Without it they follow the river picture's motion.
The river pictureThe river camera, live when it is up and light enough; otherwise the two approved recorded clips of the same creek (a 22 s cascade, an evening of 3.8 min).The screens: moving water plays faintly beneath twisting ribbons of glitter that fall through the four windows in the same direction as the ropes beside them; the water shows more as the loop builds and most at the surge. Its motion also drives the ropes when there is no sound.
The roomThe people counter on bitcube (the gallery camera, twice a second): how many people, and who has stood still for 8 s close to the ropes.A busy room or a lingerer brings the surge 30 s sooner; an empty room 30 s later. Someone standing still slows the light and gathers it toward the ropes nearest them. An empty room gets small random surges every few minutes; a busy room can bring one on, at most once a minute.

Fill my cup (one cycle in about twenty)

Takes no live input. Every rope and screen is one canvas by real height; blue and black water rises from the floor to the top with a crest and spray, holds, and drains all the way out. Its colours, brightness, intensity and speed are dials on mission control (the configurable version), not the agent's.

When something is missing

  • Dave's agent is late or the internet is down: if his choice is more than 15 minutes old, bitcube picks a notable day itself from the same record (never the same twice in an hour) and plays on with the season's colours. Each of his choices plays once; between them bitcube fills with notable days too.
  • The river camera is down or dark: the screens play the recorded clips of the same creek; the ropes follow "heard" or, without it, the clips' motion. The agent is told the camera is dark and offers notable days.
  • The people counter stops: the room is treated as empty: no shift of the surge, no lingerers.
  • The screens' GPU shaders fail: a simpler drawing of the same ribbons takes over by itself.
  • The ropes lose their signal: the sender retries every two seconds; the ropes pick the river up again when power returns.

The Status page says which of these is true right now, in words, with who to call.

What the agent is, and isn't

Dave's agent is an open-weight language model (on Modal) that reads its own journal every tick: the sensors are raw material; the journal is what makes its choices consistent and its own rather than a reaction to the last number. It is not a translator from data to light; louder water does not simply mean brighter lights. Its weights never change; its continuity is the journal, the way a person's is memory.

What it controls: the remembered day and why, the surge hour, the palette, the four knobs, the questions it asks of each clip and the small reflexes those answers may make. What it does not control: the rendering, the brightness limits, the power cap, the playlist, the calibration. Those are bitcube's and the team's.
