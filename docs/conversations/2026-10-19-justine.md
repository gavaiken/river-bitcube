## Digest: the ideas
1. **The room shapes the river.** The gallery camera senses how busy the space is, and that combines with the river input: a busy room makes the piece more turbulent and hectic, a quiet room makes it calm and still. Justine called it "the swarm" between the audience and the river: relating the movement of people to the movement of water.
2. **Memory.** Visitors trigger memories of *this* river, or of rivers in general. The water itself has been ocean, snowfall and rain; the river is just the water in it right now, but conceptually it carries those past states.
3. **What the medium can say.** It can show stillness and flow, speed and how hectic it is.
4. **Abstract vs recognisable.** It doesn't have to stay abstract. Sometimes the small displays should clearly show *a river*, so **cycle between modes**.
5. **Rocks hold the river's memory.** The water carves its shape into the rock and makes the forms around it. The river isn't just the water; it's the bed and the land that holds it.
6. **The journal has a conscience and a voice** (Dave's agent on Modal writes journal entries, in the voice of a real historical character). Should entries be shown on a display?
7. **The river has siblings.** The Nile, the Amazon and other rivers, and the meanings, rituals and cultural anchors rivers have held across cultures. Rivers as life-giving.
